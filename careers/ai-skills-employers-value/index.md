# Which AI skills should you learn?

Read job requirements critically and prioritize transferable AI skills you can demonstrate.

Updated: 2026-10-28
Publisher: ai-skills.work

## Use actual roles as evidence

Collect a few current job descriptions relevant to your interests. Highlight tasks, tool requirements, domain knowledge, and expectations around accuracy or data. Do not assume every mention of AI means model development. Some roles need practical use, others need engineering, governance, or specialist research.

- Check the seniority and responsibilities behind the title.
- Notice requirements that repeat across several employers.

## Build a transferable foundation

Useful foundations include selecting an appropriate task, writing clear instructions, checking evidence, understanding data boundaries, and communicating limitations. More technical paths may add programming, statistics, evaluation, and deployment. Select the level that matches your intended role rather than trying to learn every specialty at once.

- Pair AI skills with relevant subject knowledge.
- Include evaluation and responsible use in your practice.

## Show the skill in context

Choose a realistic project with a clear goal and observable result. Explain your inputs, checks, decisions, and limitations. Use synthetic or public material where needed. Be precise about what the tool did and what you contributed. A reviewer should be able to understand why the work is useful.

- Show a before-and-after example when appropriate.
- Avoid unsupported claims about time saved or accuracy.

## In practice: From keyword to evidence

If a role asks for AI-assisted reporting, create a small report from public data, document the source, verify the calculations, and explain your review process. This demonstrates a workflow rather than just a tool name.

## Try it yourself

Compare three role descriptions. Pick one repeated requirement and turn it into a project you could complete and explain.

## Common questions

### Is prompt engineering the only AI skill?

No. Task selection, evidence checking, data literacy, workflow design, and communication also matter. Technical roles require additional specialist skills.

### Which course should I buy?

Identify the learning outcome first. Check the syllabus, practical exercises, accessibility, independent feedback, and current price before committing.
