# Help your team start well

Plan a useful AI pilot with clear boundaries, practical training, and meaningful evaluation.

Updated: 2026-10-28

## Choose a problem the team recognizes

Ask where repetitive work causes delays or frustration. Select one bounded task with an observable output and a willing group of users. Define the baseline process before introducing a tool. A pilot should answer a specific question about usefulness, not simply demonstrate that the team has adopted AI.

- Include the people who do the work.
- Check procurement, security, accessibility, and policy requirements.

## Set boundaries and support practice

Explain which information is permitted, who reviews outputs, and which actions require approval. Provide realistic examples and time to practice. Make it easy to report errors without blame. Training should include limitations and recovery, not just the successful demonstration.

- Assign an owner for the workflow and its documentation.
- Provide an alternative when the tool is unsuitable.

## Measure the whole workflow

Compare output quality, correction effort, turnaround, and user experience with the baseline. Look for errors that are hard to detect. Keep evidence of both benefits and failures. Decide whether to stop, adjust, or expand based on those results, and continue monitoring after a rollout.

- Include review and maintenance time in cost estimates.
- Recheck performance when the tool or process changes.

## In practice: A bounded pilot

For two weeks, a small team uses an approved tool to draft internal meeting summaries. A person checks each summary. The team records correction time, missing actions, and usefulness before deciding whether to continue.

## Try it yourself

Write a one-page pilot brief with a task, owner, permitted data, review process, success measures, and stopping conditions.

## Common questions

### Should AI training be the same for everyone?

Everyone benefits from basic literacy, but practical training should reflect their tasks, authority, and data responsibilities.

### What is a good success metric?

Use measures linked to the task, such as verified output quality and total effort. Usage counts alone do not demonstrate value.
