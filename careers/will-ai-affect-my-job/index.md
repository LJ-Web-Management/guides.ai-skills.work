# Will AI affect my job?

Assess how AI could change the tasks in your role without treating uncertain forecasts as personal predictions.

## Look at tasks, not just job titles

A job combines many activities: preparing information, communicating, making decisions, building relationships, and taking responsibility. AI may assist some tasks while leaving others largely unchanged. List what you actually do in a normal week. A broad claim about an occupation is not a reliable prediction about your particular employer or future.

- Record time spent on each task.
- Separate routine production from judgment, trust, and responsibility.

## Understand exposure versus replacement

Research on AI exposure often estimates which tasks a technology could perform or assist. It does not establish whether an employer will adopt it, whether adoption is economical, or whether a job will disappear. Quality requirements, regulation, customer expectations, and organizational choices all matter. Read the definitions behind a forecast before using its headline.

- Treat scenarios as possibilities rather than promises.
- Check the country, sector, and date of the evidence.

## Turn uncertainty into a useful conversation

Identify one task where AI could help and one capability your team still needs from people. Ask your manager how tools are being evaluated and what training is available. Build a practical plan around skills you can demonstrate. No online quiz or single score can reliably predict your personal job security.

- Track changes in actual responsibilities and hiring requirements.
- Keep reviewing your plan as your workplace changes.

## In practice: A task-level view

A support role might use AI to draft routine replies, while people still handle exceptions, complex complaints, and relationship repair. The outcome depends on service design and quality expectations, not just the ability to generate text.

## Try it yourself

List five tasks in your role. Mark each as suitable for assistance, needing close review, or dependent on human responsibility. Write one learning action for the next month.

## Common questions

### Can a quiz tell me if AI will take my job?

No. These quizzes assess selected knowledge. They do not predict employment outcomes or evaluate your employer’s plans.

### Does AI exposure mean redundancy?

No. Exposure describes potential task effects. Employment outcomes depend on adoption, demand, organizational decisions, and other factors.
