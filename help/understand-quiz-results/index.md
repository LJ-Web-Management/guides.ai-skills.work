# What your quiz result means

Understand completion, answer accuracy, explanations, and the limits of a short knowledge quiz.

## Read completion and accuracy together

Completion tells you how much of the quiz you answered. Accuracy tells you how many answered questions were correct. Read both values together. A perfect result from one answered question is much less informative than a completed attempt, and the page labels incomplete attempts clearly.

- Skipped questions are listed separately.
- No overall mastery claim is inferred from a partial attempt.

## Use explanations to find the next lesson

Every question has an explanation of the intended answer. Compare the explanation with your thinking and revisit the relevant guide. A wrong answer is useful feedback about one concept, not a judgment about your ability to learn. Practice applying the idea to a different situation.

- Review correct answers too if you guessed.
- Look for assumptions that affected your choice.

## Understand what the quiz does not measure

These short quizzes sample selected concepts. They do not establish professional competence, grant a credential, or predict job security. Results can be affected by guessing, incomplete answers, familiarity with wording, and the limited number of questions. Use practical exercises and feedback alongside them.

- Do not use the score for hiring or employment decisions.
- Treat improvement as a prompt for further practice.

## In practice: A useful interpretation

You missed a question about checking citations. Read the fact-checking guide and verify three real claims. That practice is more informative than repeatedly selecting the remembered answer.

## Try it yourself

After a quiz, choose one explanation and write a new example that demonstrates the same principle.

## Common questions

### Does a high score make me an AI expert?

No. It indicates performance on a small set of knowledge questions. Expertise requires broader understanding, practice, and appropriate evaluation.

### Does the career quiz predict my job risk?

No. It checks understanding of career-related concepts and does not predict anyone’s employment outcome.
