# Use AI to become a better learner

Create explanations, practice questions, and study routines while developing your own understanding.

## Ask for an explanation you can test

Tell the assistant your current level and ask it to explain a concept using a concrete example. Ask which parts of the explanation are simplified. Then check the idea against a trusted textbook, course, or official reference. A helpful analogy can build intuition, but every analogy has limits.

- Ask for definitions before adding complexity.
- Request examples and counterexamples.

## Practice recalling, not just reading

Reading a polished answer can feel like understanding. Close the answer and explain the concept yourself, solve a similar problem, or answer a practice question. Ask the assistant to give feedback on your attempt rather than immediately provide the solution. Check its feedback when the subject requires exact answers.

- Try the problem before requesting hints.
- Space practice over several sessions.

## Build a study routine around your goal

Choose a small learning outcome and schedule practice that demonstrates it. Ask for a plan that fits the time you actually have. Keep track of mistakes and revisit weak areas. Follow your school or training provider’s rules about AI use and acknowledge assistance when required.

- Use AI to adapt practice, not replace your own work.
- Revisit difficult topics after a delay.

## In practice: A useful tutoring prompt

I am learning spreadsheet percentages. Give me one beginner problem without the answer. After I respond, check my calculation, explain any mistake, and give me a similar problem.

## Try it yourself

Read one lesson, close it, and explain its main idea in three sentences. Reopen it and correct anything you missed.

## Common questions

### Can AI replace a teacher?

AI can provide practice and explanations, but teachers bring context, feedback, responsibility, and knowledge of the learner that a tool may not have.

### How do I know I learned something?

Try explaining or applying it without looking at the answer. Correct performance on a new example is stronger evidence than familiarity with the wording.
