Find the right tool for the job

Choose an AI assistant by task, privacy, evidence, and cost rather than popularity.

THE USEFUL BIT

01

Define what a good result looks like

Describe the task before shopping for a tool. Do you need a source-backed research summary, a spreadsheet explanation, an image, or help drafting code? List the inputs, expected output, and acceptable error level. A tool that produces a polished paragraph may be a poor fit for a task that needs traceable calculations.

  • Separate essential features from conveniences.
  • Check file formats, accessibility, and export options.
02

Compare with the same realistic examples

Try two or three tools on the same non-sensitive task. Check accuracy, ease of correction, and whether the tool shows usable sources. Test a difficult example as well as an easy one. Read the current product documentation because features, limits, and prices change. A free tier can be sufficient for learning, but its limits may differ from a paid plan.

  • Score results using your own quality checklist.
  • Include the time spent correcting the output.
03

Review privacy and ongoing costs

Check whether your organization permits the product, what happens to uploaded information, and which settings control retention or training use. Do not assume a paid account automatically meets your obligations. Consider subscriptions, usage-based charges, limits, and the effort required to move your work elsewhere.

  • Use approved accounts for workplace information.
  • Set a small budget and review actual usage before expanding.
IN PRACTICE

A fair comparison

Ask two assistants to summarize the same public policy into five bullets. Check coverage, factual accuracy, source traceability, and editing time. The most confident answer does not automatically win.

YOUR TURN

Try one small thing

Make a checklist with five requirements for one real task. Test one approved tool against it using public or fictional information.

A few common questions

Do I need a paid AI tool?+

Not necessarily. Start with a free option that meets your task and privacy requirements. Upgrade when a specific limitation makes the cost worthwhile.

Which AI tool is best?+

There is no universal best tool. Suitability depends on the task, current features, privacy requirements, and your ability to verify the output.
