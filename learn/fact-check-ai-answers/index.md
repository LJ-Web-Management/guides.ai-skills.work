# Trust the evidence, check the answer

Spot AI hallucinations and verify citations, calculations, and important claims.

Updated: 2026-10-28
Publisher: ai-skills.work

## Recognize what needs checking

An AI hallucination is a generated statement presented as fact that is unsupported or incorrect. It may be a fabricated citation, a wrong date, or a plausible explanation built on a false assumption. Detailed wording and confident tone are not evidence. Mark names, numbers, quotations, legal claims, and current information for verification.

- Separate facts from suggestions and opinions.
- Prioritize errors that could change a decision.

## Go back to the original source

Open each important reference and check whether it exists, supports the exact claim, and applies to the right date and context. Prefer primary sources such as official documentation, original research, and the organization responsible for a policy. Asking another chatbot can generate another opinion, but it does not replace checking evidence.

- Read beyond a search snippet or summary.
- Check whether a source describes correlation, a forecast, or a demonstrated result.

## Verify numbers and document uncertainty

Recalculate important totals with a calculator or spreadsheet. Check units, time periods, and assumptions. If a source is missing or conflicting, mark the claim as uncertain instead of presenting it as settled. In consequential areas, pass the work to a qualified reviewer. Your review effort should match the impact of being wrong.

- Check that percentages use the correct denominator.
- Keep a short note of the source and what you verified.

## In practice: A convincing mistake

An assistant says a training budget fell by 25%, from 100 to 80. The figures may be correct, but the reduction is 20%. Recalculating catches an error that fluent wording can hide.

## Try it yourself

Take a generated answer and highlight three checkable claims. Find an original source for each and label it supported, unsupported, or uncertain.

## Common questions

### Are AI citations always real?

No. A citation can be fabricated, misquoted, outdated, or unrelated. Open it and check the specific claim.

### Is an answer with web search automatically accurate?

No. Search can provide useful evidence, but a tool can still select poor sources or misinterpret them.
